# Retail Sales \& Customer Insights

## Project Overview

An exploratory data analytics project using the UCI Online Retail dataset to understand sales performance, product revenue, geographic trends, and customer purchasing behavior.

## Business Objectives

* Analyze monthly sales trends and identify peak periods.
* Identify products generating the highest revenue.
* Compare revenue across countries.
* Examine customer purchase frequency and repeat purchasing behavior.
* Investigate cancellations, returns, missing values, and unusual transactions.

## Tools \& Technologies

* **Python:** Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn
* **Power BI:** Interactive dashboard and KPI visualization
* **Jupyter Notebook / Google Colab:** Data exploration and analysis

## Project Workflow

1. Loaded and explored the retail transaction dataset.
2. Examined missing values, duplicate rows, cancelled invoices, and unusual quantities and prices.
3. Created a positive-sales dataset for sales analysis.
4. Analyzed revenue by month, product description, and country.
5. Explored customer purchasing frequency and customer segmentation.
6. Built a Power BI dashboard to present the findings.

## Preliminary Findings

* November 2011 was the highest-revenue month in the positive-sales analysis.
* The United Kingdom was the largest market by revenue.
* Regency Cakestand 3 Tier ranked highest among the merchandise descriptions examined.
* The preliminary customer analysis indicated a concentration of revenue among high-value repeat customers.

## Limitations \& Methodology

* The dataset covers December 2010 to December 2011; December 2011 is incomplete.
* Cancelled invoices, negative quantities, zero prices, and missing customer identifiers require careful interpretation.
* Positive-sales revenue excludes negative-quantity and non-positive-price lines, so it should not be interpreted as net revenue after returns.
* Customer metrics and segmentation should be recalculated using consistent transaction filters before being treated as final results.
* Findings describe this dataset and do not establish causation.
